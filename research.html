Research

Correlated quantum matter.

I study condensed matter physics and ultracold atoms, with a focus on the mechanisms underlying few- and many-body phenomena.

01 / RESEARCH

Superconductivity

One of my research directions is to understand the properties of superconductivity when they deviate from standard BCS theory.

Finite-momentum pairing and Higgs modes: Once finite-momentum pairing is turned on, the neutral Higgs channel can couple to the electromagnetic response. Using quantum field theory, we establish the general principles underlying this effect, including Ward identities, and derive experimental signatures of the Higgs mode.

Pair-density-wave signatures: The stability of a PDW state cannot be established within a simple mean-field treatment alone. To address this, we analyze fluctuations around the mean-field solution and derive a necessary condition for stability. We also predict several distinctive signatures of the PDW state, including a superconductivity-induced Van Hove singularity and anomalous temperature dependence.

  • Anomalous superfluid density in pair-density-wave superconductors ↗
02 / RESEARCH

2D Materials

Two-dimensional materials are rich platforms for realizing interesting emergent and correlated physics.

Twisted magic-angle graphene: We establish the basic properties of intravalley superconductivity in TBG and reveal its remarkably rich phenomenology, shaped in part by the moiré flat-band nature of the system and by the absence of inversion and time-reversal symmetry in the pairing channel. This simple ansatz is already sufficient to account for many features consistent with a growing body of experimental evidence.

Our results suggest a PDW/Kekulé superconducting order in TBG. This framework naturally explains several key experimental observations, including triplet pairing, the transition from U-shaped to V-shaped features in the density of states, and Kekulé patterns in STM. The Kekulé state also approaches the BEC regime at modest interaction strengths.

Dirac fermions in monolayer graphene: The π Berry phase is a hallmark of Dirac fermions. We identify a series of experimental signatures of this property in the presence of a weak magnetic field.

  • Persistent Friedel oscillations in graphene due to a weak magnetic field ↗
03 / RESEARCH

Quantum Simulation

AMO platforms are naturally suited for simulating quantum phenomena, especially correlated physics.

Simulation of cosmological evolution: We study analog cosmological dynamics in a nondriven, closed Bose-Einstein condensate in an optical lattice, where a post-quench evolution from an unstable to a stable state realizes counterparts of preheating, reheating, and thermalization.

Ultracold molecules and nonequilibrium dynamics: Ultracold molecules can realize even richer nonequilibrium dynamics beyond the prethermal behavior found near a broad resonance. Interactions between molecules and atoms can create atom-molecule droplets and nontrivial dynamics of atomic and molecular populations.

  • Simulating cosmological evolution by quantum quench of an atomic Bose-Einstein condensate ↗
  • Universal coherent atom-molecule oscillations in the dynamics of the unitary Bose gas near a narrow Feshbach resonance ↗
  • Droplets of bosons at a narrow resonance ↗
04 / RESEARCH

Quantum Criticality

One of my research directions is to understand the universal data associated with quantum criticality.

Time-reversal-symmetry breaking in symmetry-enriched quantum criticality: In one dimension, quantum critical systems in the BDI class can support nontrivial boundary modes protected by time-reversal symmetry. We study how these modes evolve once a time-reversal-breaking field is introduced. It induces a boundary renormalization-group flow from a nontrivial to a trivial quantum critical point, characterized by two universal signatures: the finite-size amplitude and the boundary entropy.

Nonrelativistic quantum criticality: Among transitions between quantum critical points, there is a class of nonsymmetry-breaking critical phenomena that is intrinsically nonrelativistic, with dynamical exponent z=2 as a representative example. We find that such criticality carries universal data that differ qualitatively from those of conformal field theories.

  • Universal finite-size scaling around tricriticality between topologically ordered, symmetry-protected topological, and trivial phases ↗
  • Universal finite-size amplitude and anomalous entanglement entropy of z=2 quantum Lifshitz criticalities in topological chains ↗
  • Quantum z=2 Lifshitz criticality in one-dimensional interacting fermion systems ↗
